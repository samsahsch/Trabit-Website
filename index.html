      Habit Science, built in.

      Short reads on how habits really work, with the research behind them. Tap a question.

      How long does it really take to form a habit?

      About 66 days on average, not the 21 you often hear. In the study it ranged from 18 to 254 days. Lally et al., 2010

      Does missing one day ruin a new habit?

      No. A single missed day barely made a difference. That's why Trabit streaks forgive one off day, but not two in a row. Lally et al., 2010

      Why does environment beat willpower?

      Around 43% of what you do each day runs on autopilot, cued by the same time and place. Change the cue and the habit follows. Wood, Quinn & Kashy, 2002

      Can one habit carry another?

      Yes. Tie a new habit to one you already have, like "after coffee, I stretch", and you're much more likely to follow through. Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006

      Does tracking actually help?

      Yes. Across 138 studies, keeping an eye on your progress was one of the most reliable ways to change behavior. Harkin et al., 2016

      Why do Mondays feel like a fresh start?

      New weeks, months, and birthdays make people more likely to start working on goals. Use them. Dai, Milkman & Riis, 2014
